# 01 — Discovery Recap

Slides 4–6 of the deck. These three slides were carried over from the Phase 1 "Fundraising/Storytelling Toolkit Project | Discovery" deliverable and restate the problems the narrative transition is meant to solve. Each follows the same structure: a **current state**, a **desired state**, and an **opportunity** statement.

---

## 1. Voices | Audiences — DIVERSIFICATION

### Current interactions
- Institutional voice is predominant.
- Biocultures are described and illustrated, programs explained and Partners mentioned; all by an institutional voice.
- Most communications seem intended for donors and supporters, except for social media and MAPS.

### Voice and Audience diversification (desired)
- Institutional voice sets the tone, harmonizes other voices.
- Biocultures, Partners, individuals within their communities join the collective voice.
- Storytelling allows audiences' immersion and deeper connection.
- Through different channels, audiences also diversify throughout and beyond psychedelics.

### Opportunity
> Harmoniously diversify and include more IMC Fund voices, and reach a wider, balanced spectrum of audiences.

### Visual on the slide
Two radial "voice maps" side by side. Each has the IMC Fund mark at the centre and concentric arcs of blocks radiating outward (Board → Biocultures → Partners → Elders/communities → audiences). In the **current** map only the inner arc is filled dark and everything outward is white or grey (silent). In the **desired** map, blocks across all arcs are filled dark, showing many voices active at every tier.

## 2. Visual Components — UNVEILING REAL VIBRANCE

### Currently (images on the left)
- Images of people, medicines and territories have been stylized (by basically turning them into monochromatic, often blurred versions of their originals). Design-wise reasoning apart, both effects — depriving color and light, and blurring — carry strong underlying meaning, and so does opening windows to light, color and life.
- Images used in critical sections of the website and some slide decks are confusing, in the sense that they do not currently pertain to the Bioculture they are contributing to describe. Such is the case of the Yoeme/Mayo Deer Dancer in the Toad section or the Wixárika group in the Peyote chapter.
- Only a few of the recent Impact Reports include information about the images used. Showing places and also faces of members of Partner Communities as generic resources to illustrate a concept might seem impersonal, hence not aligned with IMC Fund's ethos.

### Opportunity
> Create deeper, vital connections by visually unveiling the natural vibrancy and spiritual energy of IMC Fund Partners' cultures, communities and territories.

### Visual on the slide
Two before/after pairs. A rainforest scene with a scarlet macaw, and a portrait of a young person with facial paint: the left half of each is the current desaturated, blurred treatment, the right half the full-color original. An arrow points from muted to vivid.

### Redesign implications
- Do not apply desaturation, duotone or blur to photography.
- Every photo must be verifiably from the Bioculture/territory it illustrates.
- Credit and identify people and places; avoid using community members as anonymous stock.

## 3. Narrative Evolution — MANAGING TRANSITION

### Currently (images on the left)
- In context, IMC Fund's initial narrative responded to a need to approach the task cautiously, respectfully and elegantly, all of which was accomplished. In retrospect, it may seem that the vitality of Indigenous Medicines and their Biocultures was missing, but this assessment is possible thanks to a gradual narrative transformation where more vivid representations of all aspects of the organization have been consistently emerging.
- Narrative transitions respond to organizational change, and growth. They are periods of creative exploration, innovation and change. They are also, almost by nature, spontaneous and disorganized.
- The purpose of subsequent work on IMC Fund's narrative will be to embrace this task inclusively and collectively, focusing on bringing purposeful unity and expressive harmony to the diverse and complex voices of its stakeholders, connecting with a wider range of audiences and reaching further layers of support, engagement and behavioral change.

### Opportunity
> Organized, inclusive and participatory narrative evolution, with purposeful unity and expressive harmony, effectively reaching desired outcomes.

### Visual on the slide
Two Impact Report covers side by side. The 2023–2024 cover: beige, monochrome, the mark and "IMPACT Report" in muted tones. The 2024–2025 cover ("Seeding the Future"): a full-color photo of a smiling young person in Shipibo-style patterned clothing and beadwork. The pair demonstrates that the transition toward vivid imagery has already begun.

| Theme | From | To |
|---|---|---|
| Voices / audiences | One institutional voice, donor-oriented | Institutional voice harmonizing Biocultures, Partners, Elders; diversified audiences |
| Visual components | Monochrome, blurred, mismatched imagery | Vibrant, accurate, attributed imagery |
| Narrative evolution | Spontaneous, disorganized transition | Organized, participatory, harmonious evolution |

## Harmonizing four tiers of IMC Fund Voices (slides 7 and 11)

The deck organizes everyone who speaks for or with IMC Fund into four tiers, drawn as concentric arcs around the IMC Fund mark. Each tier has its own color in the diagram.

| Tier | Label on slide | Diagram color (sampled) | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **BOARD / EXECUTIVE LEADERSHIP (Institutional Voice)** | green `#5f8e43` | Sets the tone and harmonizes the other voices. Closest to the centre. |
| 2 | **BIOCULTURES** | teal `#2f7169` | The five medicine biocultures: Ayahuasca, Iboga, Mushrooms, Peyote, Toad. |
| 3 | **PARTNERS** | blue `#315c8e` | Partner organizations and communities IMC Fund funds and works with. |
| 4 | **TERRITORIES, ELDERS and COMMUNITY LEADERS** | purple `#6e54b7` | Individuals and places within the communities; the outermost, most numerous tier. |

Slide 7 labels tier 4 "ELDERS and COMMUNITY LEADERS"; slide 11 expands it to "TERRITORIES, ELDERS and COMMUNITY LEADERS".

### How the diagram reads
- Tier 1 is two large green arcs nearest the centre.
- Tier 2 is a ring of medium teal segments.
- Tier 3 is a ring of many smaller blue blocks.
- Tier 4 is a ring of many small purple blocks.
- Beyond tier 4, faint white/grey arcs represent audiences that have not yet been reached.
- All blocks are connected by thin grey "threads" back to the centre mark, foreshadowing the Common Thread idea.

### Slide 11 — the system in one view
Slide 11 ("Harmonizing four tiers of IMC Fund Voices", with a double-headed arrow) lays the four tiers out as columns over the five Bioculture rows:

| Column | What is shown per Bioculture |
|---|---|
| Board / Executive Leadership (Institutional Voice) | The IMC Fund mark in white on the dark common-thread texture. Constant across all rows. |

Dashed yellow-green vertical dividers separate the columns. The reading is: the institutional identity is the constant thread; Biocultures bring pattern; Partners bring color; territories and people bring photography.

## The Common Thread (slide 8)

Full-slide dark woven texture with a single white stitched line rising diagonally from bottom-left to top-right, like a running stitch across fabric.

Text on the slide:

> **The Common Thread**
>
> - The essential idea is that our relationships are all interwoven on a common thread.
> - In the specific context of IMC Fund: all people, the psychedelic movement and all of its constituents, Biocultures, Partners, their territories (land, ecosystems) and communities, their knowledge and wisdom and, at the origin of it all, Indigenous Medicines; all are interlaced, and therefore interdependent.

### Why it matters for the visual system
- The dark woven/grid texture that underlies every slide **is** the common thread. It is the "IMCF pattern" that each Bioculture pattern blends out of (see file 03).
- The thin connecting lines in the voice diagrams and in the implementation-example annotations are the same idea drawn as line work.
- "Interwoven", "interlaced", "textile" and "thread" are the vocabulary the redesign should keep using.

## Proposed logo evolution (slides 2–3)

Footer on both slides: *LIFT Economy | IMC Fund Storytelling Kit Project | Phase 2 | Possible evolutions of IMC Fund logo*

### The mark
- A loose spiral made of about 18 leaf/seed-shaped strokes, opening clockwise.
- Five small dots clustered at the centre (a seed/flower cluster).
- Four single leaves placed outside the spiral at the top, bottom, left and right, like cardinal points.
- Color: dark forest green, sampled at about `#36422d` on white. Used in white on dark backgrounds throughout the deck.

### The wordmark
- INDIGENOUS / MEDICINE / CONSERVATION / FUND on four lines, all caps, very wide letter-spacing, geometric sans.
- Lockup: mark on the left, wordmark on the right, vertically centred.
- A one-line horizontal variant (INDIGENOUS MEDICINE CONSERVATION FUND, with FUND in lighter weight) is used as the header on the Discovery slides.
- A small stacked variant (mark plus four short lines) is used as the header on all dark system slides.

### What "evolution" means here
The slides show the mark and lockup cleanly redrawn rather than a new symbol. The change is in weight, spacing and the cleaner leaf shapes, and in the confidence of using the mark in white over vivid patterns. Treat the current mark as the base and expect refinement, not replacement.

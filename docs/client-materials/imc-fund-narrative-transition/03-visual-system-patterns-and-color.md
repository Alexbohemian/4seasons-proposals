## How the patterns and color systems were developed (slide 12)

> **How were textile-inspired patterns and color systems developed?**
>
> Three components*:
> - Visual boards
> - "Colorscapes" (chromatic analysis)
> - Textile-inspired patterns drawn from nature
>
> \* sample images on this page are from random cultures and territories not related to IMC Fund

### 1. Visual boards
> Inspiring, brief collections of images that depict a territory, interweaving its life forms, people, culture and art as a cohesive whole.

Shown as a strip of six photos from one territory (forest, bird, grass, animal, people, rock art), i.e. flora, fauna, landscape, people and art side by side.

### 2. Colorscapes
> By blurring select images and capturing their essential colors we create chromatic abstracts and turn them into color systems.

Shown as: photo → blurred version → sampled points → three flat color swatches (purple, blue, red-orange). Blur is used here as an **analysis tool** to find dominant colors, not as a presentation treatment.

### 3. Textile-inspired patterns
> Based on natural forms or synthesized versions of endemic textile designs, we extract patterns and apply specific color systems.

Shown as: photo of grass tufts → traced yellow strokes over a grid → flat purple motif tiles → the motif reduced to a fine, dense repeat. The pattern ends up as a subtle single-color texture.

## The five patterns (slide 9)

Slide title: **Textile-inspired Patterns**. Five horizontal strips on the dark common-thread texture, each with the Bioculture name in a light serif at the left. In every strip the left portion is the plain dark common-thread texture and the right portion blends into the Bioculture's own pattern (a left-to-right blend).

| Bioculture | Pattern description | Dominant tone |
|---|---|---|
| **Ayahuasca** | Dense, angular, maze-like geometric motif reminiscent of Shipibo kené line work. | Dark green with lime highlights |
| **Iboga** | Horizontal bands of small repeated circular/oval beads separated by thin lines, like strung beadwork or raffia weave. | Near-black with dark orange/red bands |
| **Mushrooms** | Fine branching lines with nodes, like mycelium or circuit traces. | Very dark blue-green with pale blue nodes |
| **Peyote** | Repeating diamond/lozenge lattice, like Wixárika (Huichol) beadwork and yarn. | Dark olive/brown with amber |
| **Toad** | Interlocking ogee/scale pattern with small dot centres, like toad skin or woven eyes. | Deep navy blue |

```
│                          │▌│                                   │
└──────────────────────────┴─┴──────────────────────────────────┘

                    highlight colors, stacked in equal segments,
                    running from the top edge to ~80% height
```

- **Bottom-top blend (left half):** the IMCF common-thread texture at the bottom edge blends upward into the Bioculture pattern.
- **Left-right horizontal blend (right half):** the common-thread texture at the left blends rightward into the Bioculture pattern.
- **Highlights bar:** a narrow vertical bar at the centre seam, divided into five equal segments, one per highlight color. This bar becomes the signature "accent stripe" in the implementation examples.
- **Dark, base colors** and **mid-tone colors** are shown as two horizontal 5-swatch strips on the right half.

## Rules that fall out of the system

1. **Background is always the common thread.** Start from the dark woven texture; introduce a Bioculture by blending its pattern in, never by hard-swapping the background.
2. **Highlights are for accents only.** Vertical stripes, big statistics, quote marks, small icons. Never as large fills.
3. **Mid-tones carry graphics.** Cards, numbered steps, quote blocks, chart fills.
4. **Dark base colors are for fills behind text and pattern tinting.**
5. **Photography is full color and correctly attributed.** It is the fourth "voice" (territories, elders, community leaders) and is never desaturated.
6. **Pattern is subtle.** In every example the pattern reads as texture at maybe 10–20% contrast against its base, not as a bold print.

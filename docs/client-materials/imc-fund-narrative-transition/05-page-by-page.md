### Slide 5 — Discovery: Visual Components
*Source: 06 IMC Fund Fundraising/Storytelling Toolkit, 1. Discovery final pages*

![Slide 5](slides/slide-05.jpg)

> **VISUAL COMPONENTS**
> **UNVEILING REAL VIBRANCE**
>
> OPPORTUNITY:
> Create deeper, vital connections by visually unveiling the natural vibrancy and spiritual energy of IMC Fund Partners' cultures, communities and territories.
>
> Currently (images on the left)
> - Images of people, medicines and territories have been stylized (by basically turning them into monochromatic, often blurred versions of their originals). Design-wise reasoning apart, both effects –depriving color and light, and blurring- carry strong underlying meaning, and so does opening windows to light, color and life.
> - Images used in critical sections of the website and some slide decks are confusing, in the sense that they do not currently pertain to the Bioculture they are contributing to describe. Such is the case of the Yoeme/Mayo Deer Dancer in the Toad section or the Wixarika group in the Peyote chapter.
> - Only a few of the recent Impact Reports include information about the images used. Showing places and also faces of members of Partner Communities as generic resources to illustrate a concept might seem impersonal, hence not aligned with IMC Fund's ethos.

Visual: two split images (macaw in rainforest; young person with facial paint), muted on the left half and full-color on the right half, with an arrow beneath each.

### Slide 6 — Discovery: Narrative Evolution
*Source: same as slide 5*

![Slide 6](slides/slide-06.jpg)

> **NARRATIVE EVOLUTION**
> **MANAGING TRANSITION**
>
> OPPORTUNITY:
> Organized, inclusive and participatory narrative evolution, with purposeful unity and expressive harmony, effectively reaching desired outcomes.
>
> Currently (images on the left)
> - In context, IMC Fund's initial narrative responded to a need to approach the task cautiously, respectfully and elegantly, all of which was accomplished. In retrospect, it may seem that the vitality of Indigenous Medicines and their Biocultures was missing, but this assessment is possible thanks to a gradual narrative transformation where more vivid representations of all aspects of the organization have been consistently emerging.
> - Narrative transitions respond to organizational change, and growth. They are periods of creative exploration, innovation and change. They are also, almost by nature, spontaneous and disorganized.
> - The purpose of subsequent work on IMC Fund's narrative will be to embrace this task inclusively and collectively, focusing on bringing purposeful unity and expressive harmony to the diverse and complex voices of its stakeholders, connecting with a wider range of audiences and reaching further layers of support, engagement and behavioral change.

Visual: Impact Report 2023–2024 cover (beige, monochrome) beside Impact Report 2024–2025 "Seeding the Future" cover (full-color portrait).

### Slide 12 — How were textile-inspired patterns and color systems developed?
*Source: 13 Patterns-Color S Components*

![Slide 12](slides/slide-12.jpg)

> **How were textile-inspired patterns and color systems developed?**
>
> Three components*:
> - Visual boards
> - "Colorscapes" (chromatic analysis)
> - Textile-inspired patterns drawn from nature
>
> \* sample images on this page are from random cultures and territories not related to IMC Fund
>
> **1. VISUAL BOARDS**
> Inspiring, brief collections of images that depict a territory, interweaving its life forms, people, culture and art as a cohesive whole.
>
> **2. COLORSCAPES**
> By blurring select images and capturing their essential colors we create chromatic abstracts and turn them into color systems.
>
> **3. TEXTILE-INSPIRED PATTERNS**
> Based on natural forms or synthesized versions of endemic textile designs, we extract patterns and apply specific color systems.

Visuals: (1) a six-image strip of one territory; (2) photo → blurred photo → three sampled swatches; (3) grass photo → traced strokes on grid → flat motif → dense repeat.
